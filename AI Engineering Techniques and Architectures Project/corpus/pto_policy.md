# POL-001: Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy

**Owner:** People Operations | **Effective:** January 1, 2026 | **Applies to:** All regular employees of Northwind Analytics, Inc. (synthetic company used for a course project)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Northwind Analytics ("Northwind") gives employees paid time off (PTO) so that they can rest, recharge, and attend to personal matters. This policy explains how PTO is earned, how much can be carried over, how to request it, and who must approve it. It applies to full-time and part-time employees in every Northwind office (Austin, Karachi, London) and to employees who work remotely. It does not apply to independent contractors, who manage their own time under the terms of their service agreements.

PTO is separate from paid company holidays (see POL-002) and from sick leave and other leaves of absence (see POL-007). Where a local law in Pakistan or the United Kingdom gives employees a greater entitlement than this policy, the local law applies.

## 2. Eligibility

All regular full-time and part-time employees are eligible to accrue PTO from their first day of employment. Contractors, interns, and temporary agency workers are not eligible.

New employees accrue PTO from day one, but they may not take PTO during the 90-day introductory period (see POL-012, Section 4) unless the People team approves it in writing. The only usual exception is a pre-planned commitment that the employee disclosed before accepting the offer, such as a wedding or a pre-booked trip. Employees in the introductory period may still use the paid company holidays described in POL-002.

## 3. Accrual Rates

PTO accrues monthly, on the last business day of each month, at one-twelfth of the annual entitlement. The annual entitlement for full-time employees depends on length of service:

| Length of service | Annual PTO (full-time) | Monthly accrual |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 2 years | 15 days | 1.25 days |
| 2 years to less than 5 years | 20 days | 1.67 days |
| 5 years or more | 25 days | 2.08 days |

The tier increases on the employee's anniversary date, and the higher accrual rate starts with the next month-end accrual. A "day" of PTO is a standard working day of eight hours. Part-time employees accrue PTO in proportion to their scheduled weekly hours divided by 40. For example, a part-time employee who works 25 hours per week earns 62.5% of the full-time entitlement for their service tier.

Employees can see their accrued, used, and pending PTO balances in the HR system at any time. Pending requests reduce the available balance until they are approved and taken or are cancelled.

## 4. Carryover

An employee may carry over up to 5 unused PTO days from one calendar year into the next. Any balance above 5 days is forfeited on December 31. Carried-over days must be used by March 31 of the new year; days still unused on April 1 expire and are not paid out. Days that are carried over are used first when PTO is taken.

## 5. Requesting PTO and Notice Periods

Employees request PTO in the HR system and should also tell their manager directly. The amount of notice required depends on the length of the absence:

- **1 to 2 consecutive working days:** at least 3 business days of notice.
- **3 to 5 consecutive working days:** at least 10 business days (two weeks) of notice.
- **More than 5 consecutive working days:** at least 20 business days (four weeks) of notice.

For any request of 3 or more consecutive days, the employee must also share a short coverage plan with their manager that names who will handle urgent work while they are away. Requests are counted in working days; weekends and company holidays that fall inside the absence are not deducted.

## 6. Manager Approval and Blackout Periods

All PTO requires approval from the employee's direct manager. A manager should respond within 3 business days of receiving a request. If the manager has not responded after 3 business days, the employee may ask the People team to help resolve the request. A request is not approved until the HR system shows it as approved; employees should not book non-refundable travel before approval.

**Finance quarter-end blackout.** To protect the quarterly close, employees in the Finance department may not take more than 1 day of PTO during the last 5 business days of each calendar quarter (the last five business days of March, June, September, and December) without written approval from the Finance Director. Other departments may announce short team-specific blackout windows with at least 30 days of notice.

## 7. Short-Notice Requests, Exceptions, and Negative Balances

Requests that do not meet the notice period in Section 5 are treated as short-notice requests. A manager may approve a short-notice request when there is a documented business reason, such as an unexpected family event, and when coverage is arranged. A manager is never required to approve a short-notice request for leisure travel. For genuine emergencies, employees should notify their manager as soon as possible and submit the request in the HR system within 2 business days afterward.

PTO balances may not go negative as a general rule. An employee who has completed at least one year of service and who is past the introductory period may ask the People team to approve an advance of up to 2 days of PTO that has not yet accrued. Any advanced days that remain unearned at termination will be deducted from the final paycheck where local law allows.

## 8. Sick Leave Is Separate

Sick leave is not deducted from PTO. Full-time employees receive 10 paid sick days per calendar year under POL-007. Employees should not use PTO when they are ill unless they have exhausted their sick days.

## 9. PTO Payout at Termination

For employees in the United States, unused accrued PTO is paid out at termination only where required by state law. In Pakistan and the United Kingdom, accrued but untaken statutory leave is paid in line with local labor law. Forfeited carryover days and unaccrued advances are not paid.

## 10. Questions and Disputes

Employees with questions about their balance should first check the HR system and then contact their HR Business Partner. Disagreements over a denied request can be raised with the next level of management and the People team. Northwind does not retaliate against employees who ask about or use their PTO in good faith.
