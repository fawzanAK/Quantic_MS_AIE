# POL-003: Remote and Flexible Work Policy

**Owner:** People Operations with Security and Tax | **Effective:** January 1, 2026 | **Applies to:** All Northwind employees

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Northwind supports flexible work because it helps people do their best work. This policy defines the work arrangements we offer, who is eligible, and the rules for temporarily working from another location, including another state or another country. Working from a location other than the employee's approved work location can create tax, legal, immigration, and data-security obligations for both the employee and the company, which is why the rules below are specific.

This policy should be read together with POL-004 (Information Security and Data Protection), POL-011 (Business Travel), and POL-012 (Employment Types and Working Hours).

## 2. Work Arrangement Types

Each role at Northwind is assigned one of three arrangements:

- **Office-based:** the employee works from a Northwind office on a regular basis.
- **Hybrid:** the employee works from the assigned office at least 3 days per week. Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday are the team "core" in-office days.
- **Fully remote:** the employee works from an approved home location and visits an office only for planned events. A fully remote arrangement must be approved in the offer letter or by the department VP and the People team.

An employee's approved work location is recorded in the HR system. Working regularly from any other location requires a formal change of work location, which is a People team process and is not covered by the temporary arrangements below.

## 3. Eligibility for Remote and Flexible Work

To use any of the temporary arrangements in Sections 4 and 5, an employee must:

1. Be a regular full-time employee (contractors follow the terms of their agreement);
2. Have completed the 90-day introductory period;
3. Hold a role that can be done remotely and be in good standing; and
4. Have manager approval in advance, recorded in the HR system.

Employees in the introductory period are not eligible for temporary work from another state or country. Part-time employees may apply on the same basis, with manager approval.

## 4. Temporary Work from Another Location Within the Same Country

An eligible employee may work temporarily from another location in their home country (for example, a different U.S. state) for up to **20 business days per calendar year**. The employee must:

- request approval from their manager at least 10 business days before the start date;
- tell the People team the state or region so that payroll tax withholding can be reviewed (some U.S. states create a tax obligation after a short stay); and
- remain available during the team's core hours (10:00 to 15:00 in the team's time zone) and be reachable by video and chat.

If the stay in another U.S. state will exceed 20 business days in a calendar year, the arrangement is no longer temporary and needs a formal work location change reviewed by People Operations and Tax.

## 5. Temporary Work from Another Country (International Remote Work)

International temporary remote work carries more risk, so the limits are stricter:

- **Maximum duration:** no more than **20 business days (about four weeks) per calendar year** in total, across all foreign countries.
- **Advance notice:** requests must be submitted at least **30 calendar days** before the planned start date.
- **Required approvals:** the employee's manager, the department VP, the People team, and a Tax and Legal review submitted through the Security and Tax intake form.
- **Security review:** employees who have Confidential or Restricted data access (see POL-004) must complete a security review before travel.
- **Work authorization:** the employee is responsible for having the legal right to be present and to work in the destination country. Northwind does not sponsor visas for temporary remote work.
- **Reachability:** the employee must overlap at least 4 hours with their team's core hours.

**Requests longer than 20 business days.** A request for more than 20 business days (for example, six weeks, which is 30 business days) exceeds the policy limit. These requests are not approved through the normal process. They require an exception approved in writing by the department VP, the Chief People Officer, and Tax and Legal. Exceptions are rare because longer stays can create a taxable presence for the company and personal tax and social-security obligations for the employee. An employee who wants a long stay abroad should first discuss the plan with their manager and the People team, and may be offered unpaid leave or PTO instead.

**Restricted destinations.** Employees may not work from countries that are subject to comprehensive sanctions or embargoes, currently Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Russia, Belarus, and the Crimea region. Requests for these destinations are always denied.

## 6. Interaction With PTO and Business Travel

Time spent on PTO in another location does not count toward the remote work limits, because the employee is not working. Working a few hours during a vacation is not permitted unless it is approved as remote work under this policy. Business travel that Northwind has requested is governed by POL-011 and does not count toward the 20-day limits.

## 7. Equipment and Connectivity

Employees working remotely must use only Northwind-issued devices for work involving Confidential or Restricted data. The employee is responsible for a reliable internet connection and a private workspace. Information on the home office stipend is in POL-005, Section 6. Lost or stolen equipment must be reported under POL-010.

## 8. Approval Workflow Summary

1. The employee confirms eligibility and checks the destination against the restricted list.
2. The employee submits the request in the HR system and attaches the Security and Tax intake form for international requests.
3. The manager, department VP (international), People team, and Tax/Legal (international) review the request.
4. The employee receives written confirmation. Work from another location must not begin until written approval is received.

## 9. Revocation and Compliance

Northwind may end a temporary arrangement with reasonable notice if business needs change or if the employee does not follow this policy. Working from an unapproved location can lead to corrective action under POL-008.
